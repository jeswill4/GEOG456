Terms And Equations

SPEI Calculation

The Standardized Precipitation-Evapotranspiration Index (SPEI) accounts for both water supply and demand. The climatic water balance $D$ is defined as:

$$D_{i} = P_{i} - PET_{i}$$
$$\text{Balance } (D_{i}) = \text{Supply } (P_{i}) - \text{Demand } (PET_{i})$$
$$\text{ } $$

Potential Evapotranspiration ($PET$) is calculated using the Thornthwaite Equation.

$P_{i}$ is calculated as the average precipitation over the selected timescale. This is later balanced by the $\mu$ term in the z-score calculation.

Thornthwaite Equation

$$PET = 16 \left( \frac{10T}{I} \right)^{a}$$

Annual Heat Index ($I$): The sum of all 12 monthly heat indices ($i$).

Monthly Index $$i = \left( \frac{T}{5} \right)^{1.514}$$
→
Annual Sum $$I = \sum_{m=1}^{12} i_m$$

$a$: Exponent is a cubic function of $I$. Latitude is implemented in this variable to act as a weight of temperature change in the Thornthwaite equation.

Standardized Anomalies

All variables are converted to Z-scores based on the 100-year historical baseline (1926–2026) and then displayed in the map layers above. This identifies how many standard deviations a value is from the mean ($\mu$):

$$z = \frac{x - \mu}{\sigma}$$

A score of $0$ represents the historical norm. $\sigma$ is the standard deviation term, which is the amount of fluctation of $x$.

$\mu$ is the mean of the timescale selected, over the 100-year history. This balances the $P$ term which is also only based on the timescale selected.

ENSO Data Sourcing

ENSO classifications are based on the Oceanic Niño Index (ONI) from the NOAA Climate Prediction Center (1950–Present). Earlier classifications (1926–1949) utilize historical reconstructions from the NOAA Physical Sciences Laboratory. As of early 2026, conditions are Neutral following the transition from the 2023–2024 El Niño.

El Niño-Southern Oscillation (ENSO) are recurring climate pattern in the tropical Pacific with warm phases (El Niño) leading to weakened trade winds, cold phases (La Niña) leading to stronger trade winds and in between phases (Neutral).

El Niño often means wetter colder winters in the Southern US while drier and warmer winters in the Northern US and Canada. It also, supresses hurricanes in the Atlantic.

La Niña often means warmer drier winters in the Southern US and colder and stormier winters in the Northern US and Canada. Increases likelyhood of hurricanes in the Atlantic.
